Tor wallet

Tor is built in, and it stays on

Balances, prices, swaps and broadcasts all leave through an embedded Tor client. You install nothing extra and set nothing up. If Tor is not ready, Kyro stops.

The client

One app, Tor included

Kyro embeds Arti, the Tor client from The Tor Project. It starts in the background when the app opens, and the wallet shows its state.

  • The interface cannot reach any server. Only the Rust shell can, and only over the list below.
  • Kyro contacts only the servers listed in docs/NETWORK.md, and refuses every other host.
  • Only HTTPS is accepted, with one exception: the onion service for Bitcoin.
  • Your custom servers go through Tor as well.

Circuits

A circuit per blockchain

A server or an exit relay cannot link your Bitcoin lookup to your Solana lookup through the circuit, because they never share one.

Isolated by family

Each blockchain family has its own isolated circuits. Prices and swaps have their own too. All EVM networks count as one family.

Bitcoin stays inside Tor

Bitcoin lookups go to mempool’s onion service. No exit relay is involved, so no relay sees the hostname or the volume.

Retries stay on Tor

A stream refused by a closed circuit is retried on a new one. A refusal on one family no longer moves the others. It never falls back to clear.

Fails closed

No Tor, no request

If Tor is not ready within 60 seconds, the request fails with TOR_UNAVAILABLE. Nothing goes out in the clear, and there is no automatic fallback.

Leaving Tor is your decision. Kyro asks in a native system window drawn outside the interface, so a compromised page cannot turn Tor off without you.

Limits

What Tor does not do

Tor is one layer. It hides your network location. These things stay true with it on.

Servers still see lookups

The server you query sees the addresses and transactions you ask about. To avoid that, use your own node.

Blockchains stay public

Most chains publish every transfer. Tor hides your IP, and your addresses remain linked to each other on chain when you link them.

Swap providers see the order

Swap APIs see the tokens, amounts and recipient. Exolix sees your source and destination addresses, so it can link those two accounts.

Two background requests

The signed update check and the anonymous weekly count. Both go over Tor and both can be turned off.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need to install Tor or Tor Browser?

No. Kyro embeds its own Tor client, Arti, from The Tor Project. It starts in the background when the app opens.

Can I turn Tor off?

Yes. It is your choice in Settings, confirmed in a native system window, so a compromised interface cannot switch it off for you. In clear mode, servers see your IP address.

Does Tor make my transactions anonymous?

No. Tor hides who is asking. It does not hide what is asked: the server you query still sees the addresses and transactions you look up, and public blockchains stay public.

Why is Kyro slower over Tor?

Requests take three relays instead of a direct path, and some servers refuse some exit relays. Kyro retries on a new circuit, and each blockchain shows as soon as it answers.

Do my own servers go through Tor too?

Yes. When Tor is on, your custom servers use the isolated circuits of their own blockchain family.
