Privacy

Privacy policy

Kyro Wallet collects no personal data. There is no account and no analytics. Once a week, an anonymous download over Tor helps estimate how many wallets are active.

What Kyro collects

No personal data. The Kyro developers do not receive any personal data from the app: no crash reports, no identifiers, no addresses, no balances. Kyro has no user accounts and no sign-up.

Anonymous weekly count

Once a week, after you unlock Kyro and only over Tor, the app downloads a tiny file (ping.txt) from Kyro’s latest GitHub release. Nothing is sent with it: no identifier, no version, no address. GitHub’s public download counter for that file then gives an estimate of how many wallets are active each week; the only thing Kyro stores is the week it last did this. If Tor is off, it never happens. You can turn it off in Settings, Privacy, “Anonymous weekly count”.

Data on your device

Your recovery phrase and keys are encrypted with your password (Argon2id, then XChaCha20-Poly1305) and stored only on your computer. Settings, contacts and cached balances also stay on your computer. Nothing is backed up to a server or a cloud. Uninstalling Kyro and deleting its app folder removes this data; keep your recovery phrase on paper to restore your wallet.

Network requests

To show balances, prices and history, and to send transactions and swaps, Kyro contacts only the blockchain, price and swap providers listed in NETWORK.md, which also says what each one sees. These requests go through the Tor network built into Kyro by default, so the providers do not see your IP address. They do see what you ask for: the public addresses you look up, the transactions you broadcast and, for swaps, the tokens, amounts and receiving address. Their own privacy policies apply to that data. You can use your own nodes and servers instead (Settings, Networks).

The website kyrowallet.com sets no cookies, uses no trackers or analytics and makes no third-party request. Everything it loads comes from the site itself, and its usage counters are written into the pages when the site is built.

Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue on GitHub. Security issues: report them privately through GitHub security advisories.
