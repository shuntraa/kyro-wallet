Glossary

Words, in plain language

What a recovery phrase is, why gas costs money, what a Tor circuit does. The terms you meet in a crypto wallet, kept short.

All terms

ABCDEFGHLNOPRSTVX
Address
The public string you share to receive coins. Safe to give out, though it can link your activity on a public blockchain.
Address poisoning
A scam that plants a lookalike address in your history so you copy it by mistake. How to spot it.
Approval
Permission you give a contract to move a token from your account. Kyro approves the exact amount by default. An unlimited approval needs an explicit action and shows a warning.
APY
Annual percentage yield: a yearly rate of return, with compounding. In Kyro every APY is an estimate, never a promise.
BIP39
The standard that turns a random number into 12 or 24 English words. It is why a phrase can move between wallets.
Blind signing
Approving a transaction your hardware wallet cannot decode. The device shows raw data, so it protects your keys but not your consent.
Blockchain
A shared public record of transactions, kept by many computers.
Clear mode
Kyro with Tor turned off. Servers see your IP address. You can only reach it by confirming in a system window.
Custodial wallet
A wallet where a company holds the keys for you. You trust it to give your coins back. Kyro is not one.
Decoy
In Monero, other outputs mixed with your real one when you send, so an observer cannot tell which was spent.
Depeg
When a token meant to track another asset, such as a stablecoin or stETH, drifts from its target price.
Derivation path
The recipe that turns a phrase into a specific key. Two wallets using different paths show different addresses for the same phrase.
Epoch
A fixed period on a proof-of-stake network. Staking changes often take effect at an epoch boundary.
Exit relay
The last Tor relay, which connects to the server. The server sees its IP address instead of yours.
Fail closed
Stopping when a protection is missing, rather than carrying on without it. If Tor is down, Kyro sends nothing.
Gas
The fee for running a transaction on Ethereum and similar networks. It goes to the network, not to Kyro.
Hardware wallet
A small device that keeps your keys and signs on its own screen. Ledger and Trezor in Kyro.
Lending
Depositing tokens in a protocol such as Aave so borrowers can use them, for a variable rate. Contract risk applies.
Liquid staking
Staking through a protocol such as Lido that gives you a token, stETH, standing for your stake.
Network fee
What you pay the blockchain to process a transaction. It is separate from any Kyro fee.
Node
A computer that keeps a copy of a blockchain and answers questions about it. Running your own means no third party sees your lookups.
Non-custodial wallet
A wallet where you alone hold the keys. Nobody can freeze your funds or recover them for you.
Onion service
A server reachable only inside Tor, so no exit relay is involved. Kyro uses one for Bitcoin lookups.
Private key
The secret that signs transactions. Kyro derives it for each signature in the Rust core, then wipes it from memory.
Recovery phrase
12 or 24 words that restore your whole wallet. Also called a seed phrase. How to back it up.
RPC server
A server a wallet talks to in order to read a blockchain and broadcast transactions.
Seed
The secret value your keys come from. In everyday use, the same thing as your recovery phrase.
Slashing
A penalty that destroys part of a stake. Delegators on Solana, Cardano, TRON, NEAR, Sui and Aptos are not slashed today.
Slippage
The gap between the quoted swap rate and the rate you get. Your maximum slippage is the most you accept.
Stablecoin
A token designed to track a currency, such as USDC or USDT.
Staking
Supporting a proof-of-stake network with your coins in exchange for rewards. In Kyro, you delegate from your own account.
Stake pool
A group stake run by an operator, as on Cardano and NEAR.
Swap
Exchanging one coin for another from your wallet, through a provider. Kyro’s fee and the provider’s costs are included in the rate.
Token
An asset that lives on a blockchain, such as an ERC-20 or SPL token, as opposed to the chain’s native coin.
Tor
A network that hides your IP address by routing traffic through volunteer relays. How Kyro uses it.
Tor circuit
The path of three relays a connection takes through Tor. Kyro keeps a separate one for each blockchain family.
Validator
A computer that helps run a proof-of-stake network and earns rewards. It cannot spend what you delegate.
View key
A Monero key that reads incoming payments but cannot spend. In Kyro it stays on your machine.
xpub
An extended public key. It can show all addresses of an account, but cannot spend. Kyro stores xpubs for hardware accounts.
