What works on each blockchain

Privacy

Everything goes through Tor

Kyro is a Tor wallet from the first launch. Balances, prices, swaps and broadcasts leave through the built-in client, each blockchain gets its own circuit, and if Tor fails, Kyro stops.

The server sees •••.•••.•••.••• A Tor exit relay
  1. KyroYour computer
  2. 1Relay 1Sees your address, blind to the rest
  3. 2Relay 2Sees two other relays
  4. 3Relay 3Sees the request, never you
  5. NetworkSees a Tor exit

Security

Yours, start to finish

A non-custodial, open-source wallet. No account and no analytics. Only an anonymous weekly count over Tor, which you can turn off.

Keys stay on your device

Encrypted with your password and kept in the Rust core. No server has a copy.

How Kyro protects your keys

Questions

Short answers

Is Kyro Wallet free?

Yes. Kyro is free to download and use, and open source under GPL-3.0-or-later. Sending, receiving and staking cost only the network fee. Swaps from 0.7%, built into the rate. Fee details.

Is Kyro custodial?

No. Kyro is non-custodial. Your keys are created and encrypted on your computer. There is no account and no server holding your funds, so nobody, including us, can freeze, move or recover them.

Does Kyro support hardware wallets?

Yes: Ledger and Trezor, including Trezor Safe 7. Supported on Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Polygon, Arbitrum, Optimism, Base, Avalanche, Cronos, Mantle, Sonic, Flare, Kaia, Gnosis, HyperEVM, Sei, Conflux, Solana, TRON, Cardano, XRP Ledger, Litecoin, Dogecoin, Bitcoin Cash, Dash, Stellar, NEAR, Sui, TON, Cosmos Hub, Aptos, Algorand and Zcash.

Is Kyro open source? Has it been audited?

The full source is on GitHub under GPL-3.0-or-later. An internal security review has been done. No external audit has been published yet.

How can I support Kyro?

Kyro is free. Donations keep it going. You can also sponsor on GitHub.
